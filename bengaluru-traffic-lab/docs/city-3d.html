Bengaluru 3D Route & Road-Improvement Simulator

A car drive between two points on Google's real Photorealistic 3D Tiles, over an illustrative arterial-road skeleton — with a Bureau of Public Roads (BPR) volume-delay model showing how widening, resurfacing, or festival rerouting changes travel time.

← Back to the congestion-risk map & reports
What this actually is: the 23 junctions and 31 road corridors below are a hand-built, illustrative skeleton of Bengaluru's major arterials (not full street-level OpenStreetMap data — live OSM fetching wasn't reachable from this project's dev environment). Routes are drawn as straight lines between junctions, not real street centerlines. Travel-time predictions use the standard BPR volume-delay function (travel_time = free_flow_time × (1 + 0.15×(volume/capacity)4)) with illustrative baseline volumes — a citable, transparent heuristic, not a calibrated simulation (no SUMO, no real traffic counts) and not a real network traffic-assignment/equilibrium recomputation. The 3D imagery itself is real Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles of actual Bengaluru. Treat every number here as directional ("roughly how much better/worse"), not as a forecast.

Pick a start and end junction, then watch a simulated drive along the currently fastest route (by predicted travel time). Playback is time-compressed so a real ~40-minute drive plays in well under a minute.
